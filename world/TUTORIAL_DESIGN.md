# Quasar Tutorial Design

## The Last Night of Earth / Open Hatch

**Status:** Governing onboarding design; the former *Long-Dead Signal*
satellite tutorial is retired
**Scope:** Narrative, rooms, encounters, teaching sequence, choices, completion,
and recovery
**Not in scope:** Evennia implementation, final enemy numbers, or migration of
the retired prototype code

---

## 1. Tutorial purpose

Quasar's tutorial must introduce the actual game:

- six biological launch peoples;
- Background-driven starting identity;
- two independently chosen Basic trees at character creation;
- classless growth through discovery and meaningful use;
- a third Basic-or-Advanced Flex slot earned much later;
- deep automatic-rhythm combat with deliberate abilities;
- technology, magic, and psionics in the same world;
- dangerous travel and Open Hatch guest-right;
- Porter and a customer-facing Return House;
- the one-way Human Gatefall; and
- Counterlight beginning as evidence that almost makes sense.

The previous derelict-satellite tutorial was a successful Evennia learning
project, but its story, Porter rules, Human/Synth roster, and class assumptions
are no longer canon. No part of its satellite, Mara/Juno plot, parasite arc, or
room layout must be preserved.

To support six peoples without authoring six games, onboarding uses two shells:

1. **Human characters:** *The Last Night of Earth* *(working title)*, an
   ancestral dream aboard an Icarus convoy ship.
2. **Oruun, Nimri, Serev, Kaelith, and Veyari characters:** *Open Hatch*, a
   current-day emergency at a frontier wayhouse.

The two shells share one mechanical syllabus and converge on the same first
morning in Dawnline. The Human wakes in a space hotel; the native character
returns from a provisional Return House to their own room, sleeps, and wakes the
next day. Palinode Transit remains a working name rather than locked canon.

---

## 2. Experience targets

| Target | Goal |
| --- | --- |
| Critical path | 20–30 minutes |
| Complete exploration | 35–50 minutes |
| Tutorial combat | Three ordinary lessons and one forgiving climax |
| Failure | Fast local reset with explanation and replenishment |
| Completion | First morning in a Dawnline room, bound Porter, and three next leads |
| Replay | Condensed certification available after one full account completion |

The tutorial is private per character. Global help remains available, but
other players cannot enter, alter, loot, or fight inside an active tutorial.

## 3. Shared mechanical syllabus

Both shells teach the following in this order:

1. **Observe:** LOOK, room title, exits, EXAMINE, and sensory overlays
2. **Move:** cardinal/custom exits and automatic map discovery
3. **Possess:** INVENTORY, containers, TAKE, EQUIP, and item examination
4. **Speak:** TALK/ASK, response choices, and NPC state
5. **Track:** task journal, objective update, and completion feedback
6. **Fight:** target selection and automatic weapon rhythm
7. **React:** one enemy telegraph and a universal defensive response
8. **Read cadence:** Balance and Focus recovery
9. **Use identity:** one Background knack or species interaction
10. **Practice A:** use a meaningful command from the first Basic tree
11. **Practice B:** use the second Basic and create or consume one shared state
12. **Build:** inspect SKILLS, both active Basics, proficiency, and locked Flex C
13. **Interact:** use a skill or item on scenery rather than an enemy
14. **Decide:** make one stored choice with no alignment label
15. **Converge:** wake the next morning in Dawnline and understand Porter,
    recall, billing, safety, and later reorientation

No critical command is hidden. Optional commands and discoveries may reward
careful reading.

### 3.1 Background scenario palette

The tutorial recognizes how the character describes their former life and
supplies one equivalent moment. These are authoring directions, not permanent
guaranteed abilities or fixed pieces of equipment:

| Background | Tutorial expression |
| --- | --- |
| Belt Prospector | Diagnose, brace, redirect, or exploit a structural or mineral hazard |
| Political Operator | Create time or attention through protocol, leverage, testimony, or a contested truce |
| Wayhouse Raised | Interpret urgent need, invoke guest rules, or improvise emergency provisions |
| Hullborn | Recognize a ship system by sound, find a service route, or steady a frightened crew |
| Salvage Diver | Preserve evidence, recover something fragile, or cut an entry without collapse |
| Shrine Foster | Establish a meaningful threshold, identify borrowed ritual, or provide sanctuary |
| Living-World Tender | Read, calm, redirect, preserve, or safely destroy unfamiliar growth |
| Contract Ward | Expose a coercive order, coordinate labor, or endure a controlled hardship |
| Route Runner | Find an exit, protect a message, recognize pursuit, or manipulate a chase |
| Heimdall Rigger | Translate technical and magical readings, assess a load, or isolate sabotage |
| Archive Foundling | Establish provenance, recall a source, or notice a record becoming too consistent |
| Disgraced Enforcer | Read imminent violence, protect a withdrawal, or establish a surrender channel |

The mechanical lesson adapts to every legal starting Basic pair. Each tree must
receive one meaningful moment without making the Background expression depend
on that tree. The point is to use both build and identity, not confuse them.

### 3.2 People accommodation

Tutorial difficulty is equal across bodies. Oruun receive a fitted pressure
mantle with irrigated gills and access to wet rest alcoves; Nimri equipment
preserves player control over visible color speech; Serev gear fits bearer and
mantle without treating either as cargo; Kaelith receive thermal and resonance
contact fittings; Veyari rooms and suits accommodate the dorsal sensory feather-fan. These are
normal starter provisions, not racial upkeep taxes.

Shared hazard quantities are tuned to produce the same effective learning
window. Innate senses add optional evidence rather than bypassing required
lessons.

### 3.3 Build and proficiency lesson

A safe room asks the player to open SKILLS and inspect Basic A, Basic B, their
current proficiency, the next command threshold, and the visibly locked Flex C.
The tutorial does not swap either chosen tree or lend a temporary third one.

After each Basic contributes meaningfully, the interface explains why that tree
earned proficiency. It also explains:

- trees improve mainly through meaningful use, not command repetition;
- rare lessons can accelerate an already unlocked tree but are optional;
- inactive learned trees retain proficiency but grant no commands;
- replacing a Basic later requires a safe facility and modest recoverable fee;
- removing an Advanced tree later requires deeper reorientation; and
- species innates, Background knacks, and ordinary trade skills remain
  available outside active slots.

The Return House points to a supervised reorientation service after completion.
No new player pays or changes their build before demonstrating both chosen
Basics.

# Part I: Human tutorial

## 4. The Last Night of Earth

### 4.1 Narrative premise

During character creation, the Human may choose an immediate reason for entering
Dawnline: work intake, a petition, family business, flight from an obligation,
expedition registration, or a player-written equivalent. The prompt is
optional. Skipping it supplies sardonic but nonbinding default copy such as:

> Somewhere between a rumor and a bad decision, you bought passage to
> Dawnline. The fare emptied your account; the city has not yet bothered to
> notice you arrived. You have no convincing plan, no convenient way back, and
> the stubborn glint of hope frontier obituaries are forever warning against.

This is an opening circumstance, not a permanent biography, and the player may
revise it later. The Human reached Dawnline by ordinary passenger ship, paid for
a short stay in a modest space hotel, and fell asleep on their first night. The
ancestral dream occupies that sleep; the game has not secretly chosen a career
or life goal for them.

The Human character dreams from the viewpoint of a unique convoy passenger
aboard one of the ships waiting to enter Icarus. The dream may use the
character's chosen arkship heritage, a family claim, or an unidentified vessel
when ancestry is uncertain.

The dream-person is generated once per character and remains stable across
retries and later dreams. Their gender and pronouns, age, body, face, skin, hair,
voice, scars, clothing wear, berth assignment, and small personal details are
selected from compatible palettes. They are not a cosmetic mirror of the
present character and may be a man, woman, or nonbinary person. Heritage can
influence the palette without forcing resemblance. Generated details must obey
player-authored arkship and family facts, and no randomized resemblance or
difference is treated as proof against or for biological ancestry.

The one exact inheritance is mechanical: the dream-person has the player's two
chosen Basic trees at the same starting proficiency. Any bounded tutorial
proficiency earned through meaningful use survives the awakening. This lets the
player learn the commands they will actually retain while implying a personal
connection that appearance alone cannot prove.

The dream is not labelled a flashback at first. The character wakes in a labor
berth with launch alarms counting down and a task they apparently already
accepted. Their body is Human, but their present-day Background habits and
skills bleed into the ancestor's hands. If they use magic or psionics the
ancestor should not have known, the prose treats it as an instinct with no
historical name.

This is an early clue that the experience is not an objective recording.

#### Designer truth: the skill loop

For Human characters, the dream is the diegetic source of their starting skill
memory. Counterlight allows the present character's chosen practices to bleed
backward into the dream-person, including Andromedan magic or psionics the
historical passenger could not originally have known. The dream-person then
practices those abilities aboard the ship, and that memory bleeds forward into
the waking character.

There is no clean first owner of the knowledge. Character creation determines
the mechanics; the closed causal loop explains how the Human wakes knowing
them. The game should imply this through matching commands, bodily familiarity,
later dreams, and disputed family evidence rather than explaining it during
onboarding.

The ship contains visible class divisions:

- founder suites behind clean secured doors;
- expert compartments with better air and equipment;
- dense contracted-labor berths;
- sealed penal transport spaces;
- service passages used by hidden stowaways; and
- cargo holding the machines meant to reproduce ownership on another shore.

The tutorial never claims every rich passenger was evil or every laborer was
heroic. It makes inequality material and lets the player notice who receives
space, information, safety, and belief.

### 4.2 Emotional arc

1. **Routine pressure:** finish a small launch task.
2. **Misremembered life:** something ordinary has a functional contradiction.
3. **Denial:** crew explain it as contamination, stress, or sabotage.
4. **Escalation:** growth or material behaves with simple intention.
5. **Historical disagreement:** a datapad changes during examination.
6. **Competence:** the player handles a weak physical outbreak.
7. **A choice:** warn, conceal, or reinterpret the evidence.
8. **Sublime terror:** Icarus fires and the convoy enters kaleidoscopic
   translation.
9. **Awakening:** two centuries later, Porter asks whether the character slept
   badly.

### 4.3 Room plan

The Human tutorial uses **14 rooms**. Names are functional so ship heritage can
change without rewriting navigation.

| # | Room | Primary lesson/story function |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Contract Labor Berth | LOOK, exits, dream identity, class conditions |
| 2 | Shared Wash and Suit Rack | INVENTORY, TAKE, EQUIP, body/suit presentation |
| 3 | Service Spine | movement, map, launch countdown, first NPC |
| 4 | Provision Cage | containers, task item, stowaway evidence |
| 5 | Waste Recovery | first biological contradiction and EXAMINE lesson |
| 6 | Auxiliary Workshop | weapon/tool, skills and proficiency lesson |
| 7 | Expert Junction | crew dialogue and unequal access |
| 8 | Founder Lift Vestibule | secured luxury evidence; optional, never required |
| 9 | Cooling Gallery | first combat and universal reaction |
| 10 | Data and Manifest Office | changing datapad, archive evidence, Background interaction |
| 11 | Penal Transfer Lock | guest/authority choice and second fight |
| 12 | Hydroponic Reserve | adaptive colony encounter and environmental solution |
| 13 | Translation Control Annex | climax, warning choice, convoy signatures |
| 14 | Observation Well | final Gate sequence and dream completion |

Rooms 8 and part of 11 are optional. The critical path remains clear.

### 4.4 Opening

The first output should be concise:

> You wake to a launch alarm already halfway through its sentence. The berth
> above yours is empty. Someone has written **NO RETURN LOAD** on the underside
> of the frame, then tried to scrub it away.
>
> The ship says Icarus firing begins in twenty-eight minutes.

The player sees one clear prompt to LOOK, followed by natural exit and object
language. The dream never says “your ancestor” during the opening.

A dry, unidentified voice reaches the player through the berth speaker, damaged
intercom, or simply the logic of the dream. It gives concise tutorial prompts,
comments when the player ignores an obvious exit, and becomes faintly more
personable over time. It is not historically Porter and never identifies
itself. On waking, Porter's cadence should feel uncomfortably familiar without
confirming that the two voices were literally the same.

### 4.5 Counterlight encounter palette

This section is not a canonical bestiary. Each dream uses a curated
**corruption suite** drawn from that ship's mundane ecology and materials. The
suite teaches the same mechanics while allowing different vessels, later
dreams, and accessibility settings to express horror differently.

#### Misremembered life

A familiar pest, harmless culture, or maintenance organism possesses one
functional adaptation borrowed from an incompatible history. It might sense an
environment that is not present, defend an organ its species never evolved, or
perform a behavior that only makes sense in another body. The contradiction
must change what the creature can do; cosmetic mutation alone becomes visual
noise.

Teaching purpose:

- target identification;
- automatic attack rhythm;
- one readable telegraph; and
- the difference between room description and closer evidence.

#### Problem-solving colony

A distributed material—food culture, algae, mold, sealant, recycler film,
insulation, or something culturally specific to the vessel—learns one route,
obstacle, or source of warmth. It has no confirmed personhood. The player can
fight it directly or alter the room conditions through any fair active-tree,
Background, or general interaction.

Teaching purpose:

- scenery as part of combat;
- Balance and Focus abilities;
- a visible state and target-priority decision; and
- an environmental approach with several valid solutions.

#### Borrowed function

An organism or material begins performing another category's purpose: a wall
seals a wound, a root anticipates a hand, a filter develops hunger, or a pest
tries to complete a maintenance procedure. This encounter can be a weak enemy,
an interruptible process, or a hazardous objective rather than another body to
kill.

Teaching purpose:

- use a Background or active-tree technique;
- switch targets or interrupt an action; and
- show that Counterlight creates rules, not a catalog of mutations.

#### Translation knot

The climax briefly stabilizes two or three contradictions the player has
already observed. It may manifest as an entity, a room-scale process, or a
control system trying to obey mutually exclusive histories. It is weaker than
its appearance suggests, combines familiar telegraphs, and provides abundant
recovery.

Teaching purpose:

- combine basic attack, reaction, state, and active-tree ability;
- protect one control or crew objective; and
- demonstrate competence before the narrative overwhelms certainty.

### 4.6 The changing datapad

The Manifest Office contains a wall datapad. The first reading says:

> BIOLOGICAL CONTAMINATION: NONE REPORTED

While the player remains in the text, that line becomes:

> BIOLOGICAL CONTAMINATION: REPORTED / DISMISSED

On reread it may say:

> BIOLOGICAL CONTAMINATION: YOU ALREADY READ THIS

Only the character sees the third form. A nearby crew member sees a routine
manifest. The game does not label the event Counterlight.

The original player-authored report, if they make one later, is never silently
rewritten. Horror creates system contradictions around player writing rather
than editing it.

### 4.7 Class history in the dream

Optional evidence establishes convoy composition:

- a founder household shipped art, private water, and a contract archive;
- expert staff received individual crash couches;
- labor berths list rotating hot-bunk assignments;
- penal transport records use labor values instead of professions;
- a provision count exceeds the authorized passenger manifest, suggesting
  stowaways; and
- a security message orders crew not to search certain cargo until after
  translation, because missing the firing window matters more.

No lore dump lists the entire social thesis. Players assemble it from rooms,
objects, and people.

### 4.8 Stored dream choice

At Translation Control, the character chooses how the dream-self handles the
evidence:

1. **Warn openly:** broadcast that the biological contamination is abnormal.
2. **Report through authority:** send evidence to expert/security channels.
3. **Protect the vulnerable first:** direct the warning to labor, penal, and
   hidden-access compartments.
4. **Conceal it:** avoid panic and keep the ship on schedule.

The choice does not determine whether the historical ship arrived. It stores a
dream-response flag used by later dreams, Human faction dialogue, and
Counterlight interpretation. There is no good/evil score.

### 4.9 Gate sequence

After the climax, normal combat UI fades but text remains accessible. The
Observation Well shows:

- Earth reduced to an impossible curve behind the ship;
- Icarus's aperture as layered bands of color with no stable order;
- convoy signatures stretching, doubling, or vanishing;
- one ship arriving on instruments before it enters;
- a missing vessel displaced along a direction the display cannot name; and
- the player's reflection completing a movement they decided not to make before
  returning to normal.

The sequence ends with:

> The Gate fires you toward a galaxy that has never built a door for you.
>
> You wake before the ship learns whether the calculation was right.

# Part II: Native tutorial

## 5. Open Hatch

### 5.1 Narrative premise

The native character begins with a Personal Porter already in inventory and
bound to them. Porter cheekily initiates the first LOOK prompt, explains only
what the player currently needs, and reacts to needless risk without narrating
the character's feelings.

Instead of a newcomer-to-Dawnline premise, the native player may optionally say
why Dawnline is their current base, why they accepted this wayhouse watch, or
why they intend to return. Skipping the prompt supplies adaptable default copy
such as:

> You keep a room in Dawnline because even wanderers need somewhere for their
> bills to find them. This watch was supposed to pay for another quiet week.
> The frontier has always regarded “quiet” as a negotiable term.

The copy is nonbinding and may be revised later.

The character is staying or working at **Lone Lamp Wayhouse**, a working label
for a small mixed-culture shelter on a dangerous local route. Before the
emergency, its keeper formally delegates the character one watch of threshold
authority and records that delegation in the guest ledger. The reason adapts to
Background: routine employment, repayment of hospitality, community service,
apprenticeship, or a voluntary favor. The keeper is then injured or occupied
maintaining life support when a damaged courier arrives, declares peaceful
intent, and invokes Open Hatch.

The courier is pursued by a claim-recovery crew that says the guest stole a
survey instrument and caused a death. The guest says the instrument contains
evidence of an unsafe route and the pursuers intend to erase it. Both claims
contain truth.

As acting keeper, the character must provide immediate survival aid. Shelter
does not settle ownership, guilt, or what happens after the emergency.

### 5.2 Room plan

The native tutorial uses **12 rooms** shared by all five peoples.

| # | Room | Primary lesson/story function |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Guest Bunk | LOOK, people-specific senses, Background prompt |
| 2 | Common Table | TALK, Open Hatch custom, mixed technology/magic |
| 3 | Threshold Hall | arrival, guest declaration, first choice |
| 4 | Pressure and Atmosphere Plant | equipment, hazard, repair item |
| 5 | Supply Cage | inventory, containers, task journal |
| 6 | Workshop Alcove | skills and proficiency lesson |
| 7 | Lamp Gallery | map, route evidence, ranged threat |
| 8 | Outer Gantry | first combat and telegraph |
| 9 | Shelter Yard | multi-target fight and Background technique |
| 10 | Guest Clinic | examine courier, compare evidence |
| 11 | Beacon Chamber | climax, environment, Porter connection |
| 12 | Return Pad | decision and teleport to Dawnline |

### 5.3 People-specific evidence

All variations are optional evidence, not exclusive critical commands:

- **Oruun:** Current-Sense detects pursuit through the gantry and an irregular
  vibration in the guest's instrument.
- **Nimri:** Spectrum Reading catches erased thermal writing and a pursuer's
  involuntary color-language response.
- **Serev:** Imprint preserves the arrival declaration for comparison after
  testimony changes.
- **Kaelith:** Resonance finds stress deliberately introduced into the beacon
  mount.
- **Veyari:** Presence Sense distinguishes broad panic from rehearsed outrage
  without reading anyone's private thought.

Groups are not present inside the private tutorial, so each critical conclusion
also has a tool or dialogue route.

### 5.4 Threats

The first enemies are claim drones or hired pursuers, depending on player
settings and desired lethality. They teach ordinary frontier combat without
making every danger Counterlight.

The climax combines:

- a hostile recovery leader;
- an overloaded beacon;
- a guest who may flee if abandoned;
- one Open Hatch obligation; and
- a choice between destroying, surrendering, copying, or carrying the survey
  instrument.

There may be one subtle Counterlight discrepancy in the instrument's timestamps,
but the tutorial's concrete conflict is ownership and safety.

### 5.5 Stored Open Hatch choice

The player chooses a defensible action:

- uphold shelter and carry the guest to Dawnline;
- uphold immediate shelter, then surrender the guest under witnessed terms;
- protect the evidence while allowing the guest to leave separately;
- negotiate a temporary truce and demand review; or
- betray guest-right after understanding the consequence.

The world stores the exact event, not an alignment score. Later waykeepers,
claim agents, and the guest may remember it.

# Part III: Shared convergence

## 6. First morning in Dawnline

The two stories do not end in the same building. They converge in time, mood,
and playable state: each character wakes the following morning in a private
room in Dawnline with Porter bound, their real equipment present, and the city
waiting outside.

### 6.1 Human hotel wake

The Human wakes in the modest space-hotel room they rented after arriving by
passenger ship. Cheap station dawn leaks through a programmable window or
lighting strip. Their luggage and selected Background equipment are where they
left them; no dream item crossed forward.

A Personal Porter rests on the bedside surface or fitted charging mount,
already registered and bound. This is the Human character's first literal
possession of Porter, whatever the dream voice may have sounded like. Porter
opens with a line such as:

> “Good morning. You slept badly enough to generate paperwork. On the brighter
> side, you remain in Dawnline, your account remains nearly empty, and none of
> the walls are currently remembering you back.”

During hotel check-in, the character accepted Dawnline's standard newcomer
safety registration. The hotel received the sealed Personal Porter for scheduled
morning activation, so it did not appear in the dream and was not physically in
the character's possession before waking. Character creation clearly discloses
that this registration binds non-permadeath protection by default.

The hotel is a working concept rather than a locked name. It should feel
temporary but not degrading: the first fragile foothold of someone who has
arrived with little money and unreasonable hope.

### 6.2 Native return and sleep

The native character completes the wayhouse route through a provisional transit
pad and materializes at a Dawnline Return House. Porter comments on the guest,
evidence, or missing property without announcing a moral verdict.

After immediate reports or medical needs are handled, the character returns to
their own Dawnline room. Depending on Background and heritage, this may be a
rented hotel room, work berth, family room, shrine cell, crew bunk, or similarly
modest private space. The safe trip home is concise rather than another maze.

The character secures any surviving evidence, sleeps, and wakes the next
morning. Porter greets them with a callback to the Open Hatch choice. This puts
both tutorial paths at the same in-world morning without pretending the Human
also arrived through a Return House.

### 6.3 Shared room state

Both rooms provide the same mechanical functions with different descriptions:

- a bed and reconnect point;
- fitted storage and Background equipment;
- a charging or resting place for Porter;
- a window, public feed, or sensory view of Dawnline;
- map and journal access;
- a private place to review the two-Basic build; and
- a clear exit into the same opening district.

The room is the character's immediate safe foothold, not necessarily permanent
housing. Better berths, apartments, ship quarters, and communal homes remain
early progression.

### 6.4 Porter lesson

Porter explains and demonstrates:

- TALK PORTER;
- the registered Return House;
- coverage status;
- why the watch cannot cross interstellar routes;
- why ships and rescue still matter;
- how combat and interference interrupt recall; and
- how to rebind at another Return House;
- that overdue service fees cause reminders and advertisements, not loss of
  rescue protection; and
- that only deliberate unbinding enables permanent character death.

The tutorial does not give the player a free emergency teleport from every
future site. Porter shows coverage and the registered Return House through the
device interface rather than teleporting the player again merely for a lesson.
Porter distinguishes teleport coverage from character safety: a blocked recall
may mean delayed rescue, injury, cost, or waking somewhere inconvenient, but a
bound character is not permanently deleted.

Binding is a redundant continuity-service record, not a condition stored only
inside the wearable. Losing, breaking, or having the physical device stolen can
remove immediate communication and recall controls, but cannot silently unbind
the character. Outside coverage, recovery may occur offscreen, after delay, or
through another plausible rescue route.

The unbinding explanation is concise and serious beneath Porter's humor. It
does not expose the final confirmation phrase during an ordinary tutorial
prompt; it directs interested players to explicit help. No missed payment,
empty wallet, failed recall, or tutorial choice can unbind Porter.

Billing reminders are rate-limited, can be snoozed, never appear during combat
or focused scenes, never obscure commands or accessibility output, and stop
escalating at comic nuisance. Poverty is not an accessibility penalty.

### 6.5 Three next leads

Before leaving their room, every player chooses or records interest in one of
three leads:

1. **Fight:** a paid hunt or security incident near Orison
2. **Explore:** an unverified survey discrepancy tied to the Unverified Line
3. **Work:** mining, salvage, crafting, politics, or a Heimdall material need

The choice does not lock content. It gives the player a clear first session
after onboarding.

### 6.6 Completion state

Completion awards:

- Background starter equipment;
- bound Personal Porter registration confirmed—received on waking for Humans,
  already carried by native characters;
- Dawnline room and fitted storage;
- current two-Basic build saved as the first preset;
- tutorial decision flag;
- tutorial dream flag for Humans;
- map access and journal categories;
- a modest supply package; and
- the chosen next lead.

## 7. Counterlight truth and safety rules

### 7.1 Designer canon for the Human dream

The dream is causally active.

Physical Counterlight was not present on the convoy in the original linear
history. Repeated dreams two centuries later supplied Counterlight with a path
into the past. The biological contradictions, adaptive colonies, changing
datapad, and Gate-field translation knots therefore became physically real
aboard at least some convoy ships. They arrived in Andromeda with Humanity.

This does not mean every dream detail is accurate. Counterlight combines
ancestral memory, records, inherited story, current fear, and rejected history.
The tutorial is true enough to cause consequences and unreliable enough to
remain a mystery.

### 7.2 What the player should infer initially

- Humans crossed through a knowingly one-way Gate.
- The convoy was unequal.
- Some ships did not arrive correctly.
- Something may have been wrong before arrival.
- The current character has dreams that feel more specific than folklore.

The player should not yet know:

- the dreams caused the past infestation;
- Icarus broke after the convoy;
- missing ships survived elsewhere;
- whether a vast presence notices Human expansion; or
- what Heimdall will do to the causal wound.

### 7.3 Player-authored text

Counterlight never silently rewrites player biographies, journals, messages,
or custom ship logs. It may create a disputed system copy beside an original,
show an NPC remembering something else, or make an instrument disagree. The
player's own words remain recoverable and clearly attributable.

### 7.4 Horror accessibility

Tutorial settings should support:

- reduced mutation detail;
- reduced eye/body horror;
- no flashing kaleidoscope animation;
- text-only Gate transition;
- audio cue substitution;
- screen-reader-safe telegraphs; and
- content notes before the Human dream's biological horror.

Reduced detail must preserve commands, enemy states, and clues.

## 8. Combat teaching specification

### 8.1 Forgiving numbers

- Tutorial enemies are tuned below ordinary opening-world enemies. An alert
  player should not be defeated even after several imperfect reactions.
- Defeat should require sustained refusal to react, prolonged inactivity, or a
  deliberate attempt to test the boundary—not one misunderstood command.
- Recovery items are plentiful and explained.
- Failed reactions cause readable but modest consequences.
- The climax uses fewer simultaneous demands than a normal early boss.
- Background abilities materially help but are never required for recovery.

### 8.2 Required combat proof

Before completion, analytics should confirm that the player has:

- initiated or answered an attack;
- allowed at least one automatic weapon attack;
- responded to a telegraph;
- used one Balance ability;
- used one Focus ability or understood why their build lacks one;
- used a meaningful technique from Basic A;
- used a meaningful technique from Basic B;
- created or consumed one visible state;
- changed or confirmed a target; and
- recovered from damage.

If a player bypasses story combat through a valid social or environmental
solution and the required analytics remain unproven, Porter runs a two-minute
combat certification from the character's private Dawnline room the next
morning. It is required only for the missing mechanical proof and disappears
when that proof already exists. The story continues to honor the noncombat
solution; the projection is interface certification, not a retroactive fight or
moral correction.

### 8.3 Defeat recovery

Human defeat violently wakes the dream-person in the opening labor berth. The
entire dream instance returns to its initial state: encounters, carried dream
items, temporary proficiency awards, and the unfinished dream choice reset.
The same unidentified voice resumes guidance without revealing that Porter
historically existed on the ship. Stable details of the generated dream-person
do not reroll.

Native defeat returns the character to the wayhouse clinic after an NPC rescue.
The world records no shame or permanent loss.

Repeated defeat offers:

- explicit command hints;
- replenished consumables;
- optional enemy weakening;
- a route around optional combat; and
- global help.

## 9. Privacy, replay, and cleanup

- Each tutorial is a complete private instance tied to Character ID.
- The dream-person generation seed is stored before the Human instance opens;
  reconnects and full dream resets cannot reroll it.
- Disconnect preserves room, enemies, choices, and inventory state.
- Completed tutorial proficiency and completion rewards commit in one
  idempotent transaction. A reset rolls back uncompleted dream gains; reconnect
  and retry cannot duplicate completed proficiency.
- Completion cannot duplicate permanent rewards.
- A character may revisit Human dream records later, but not re-enter tutorial
  geometry as a farming zone.
- After one full account completion, later characters may choose a condensed
  certification that preserves people- and Background-specific choices.
- The condensed Human path still shows the Gate sequence and stores a dream
  response; the condensed native path still resolves Open Hatch.
- Staff recovery can reset an incomplete tutorial without deleting the
  character.
- Instance cleanup occurs only after completion state and reward state are
  durably stored.

## 10. Content and implementation boundary

This document intentionally replaces the previous tutorial design while
leaving implementation migration for later planning.

The future implementation pass must explicitly identify and retire:

- old satellite room builders;
- Mara and Juno state;
- parasite encounters;
- derelict-specific oxygen assumptions;
- old Porter binding and departure logic;
- old tutorial tests and fixtures;
- Human/Synth-only creation branches; and
- career/discipline tutorial hooks.

Do not attempt to preserve obsolete code behavior by bending this narrative.
The new tutorial is the product requirement; the old tutorial is reference
material for what the team learned about instancing, recovery, mapping,
equipment, journals, and testing.
